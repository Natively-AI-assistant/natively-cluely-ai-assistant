# Brindlewatch Support Policy Handbook

Version 2.0 | Effective 10 March 2025
Owner: Ignatius Varga-Lindqvist, Support Operations Manager | Next scheduled review: March 2026

## 1. About this handbook

One place for the rules agents reach for most often on a call: who we can talk to about an account, and when money goes back to a customer. Product how-to lives in the knowledge base. Where the handbook is silent, ask your Team Lead before you answer the customer.

## 2. Verifying callers

Ask the caller for 2 of the 4 identifiers: full name on the account, login e-mail address, billing postal code, last four digits of the payment card.

A caller who struggles may have up to three attempts. After the third, pass the call to your Team Lead.

Household members. A spouse, or another adult living at the billing address, who passes verification may be helped as if they were the account holder.

Password resets. Reset links normally go to the e-mail on file. If the caller cannot get into that mailbox, the agent may send the link to an alternate e-mail address the caller provides, once verification is passed. Note the address on the ticket.

## 3. Subscription refunds

Annual plans. Full refund if the customer asks within 45 days of the charge, on a first purchase or a renewal.

Monthly plans. On cancellation, refund the unused days of the current month pro rata.

Refunds reach the customer in 10 to 14 business days.

Approval. An agent may approve a refund of up to $250 without asking anyone. Larger amounts go to the Team Lead.

## 4. On the call

- Use the customer's name once at the start and once at the end; more than that sounds scripted.
- Do not leave a caller on hold for more than 2 minutes without coming back to them.
- Summarise what was agreed before closing, and tell the customer what happens next and when.
- If the customer is upset, let them finish before you answer. Do not match their volume.

## 5. Wrap-up notes

Every ticket needs three lines: what the customer wanted, what was done, what is still open. Write them so that a colleague who picks up the next contact does not have to ask the customer to repeat anything.
