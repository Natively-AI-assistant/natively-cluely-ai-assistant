# Interview Scorecard: Engineering Lead, Ingestion Platform

Requisition ENG-2026-0417 | Scorecard version 2 | 4 September 2026

Owner: Idris Halvorsen-Peake (hiring manager) | Reviewed by: Leopoldine Achterberg, Head of Talent

## How to use this scorecard

Every interviewer completes one scorecard per conversation and submits it before reading anyone else's. Rate all four dimensions. Where a conversation produced nothing on a dimension, write "no evidence" in the notes and rate what you did hear; do not guess.

Use past-tense behavioral prompts ("Tell me about the last time...") rather than hypotheticals. Record what the candidate says they did, not what their team did.

## Rating scale

- 1 - Does not meet: no relevant evidence, or evidence of the opposite.
- 2 - Partially meets: some evidence, with gaps an onboarding plan would have to cover.
- 3 - Meets: clear, specific evidence at the level the role needs.
- 4 - Exceeds: evidence beyond the level of the role; could teach others.

## Dimensions and weights

- Technical depth in streaming systems carries 35% of the weighted score.
- People leadership carries 30% of the weighted score.
- Ownership and operational judgment carries 20% of the weighted score.
- Communication carries 15% of the weighted score.

A candidate advances to the next stage when the weighted score is 3.0 or higher and no dimension is rated 1.

## Must-have competencies

Each must-have needs its own evidence. A strong showing on one does not make up for a missing one.

- Streaming platform operations: has run a log-based streaming platform in production and can describe a failure they diagnosed themselves.
- Direct people management: at least 2 years with line responsibility, including hiring, and has handled at least one underperformance case.
- Incident command: has led the response to a customer-facing incident and the review afterwards.
- Explaining trade-offs: can explain a design trade-off to a non-specialist in under two minutes.

## Nice-to-have competencies

These add to a profile. They are never a reason to reject.

- Telematics or IoT device data.
- Change data capture pipelines.
- Cloud cost management for data infrastructure.
- Multi-region, active-active deployments.

A candidate who shows three or more of the nice-to-have competencies may be marked "strong profile" in the summary line.

## Evidence standard

A claim counts as verified when the candidate can state their own role, the size of the effort, one decision they personally made, and a measurable result. When an answer stays at "we", ask for the candidate's own part.

If something said in the interview differs from the resume, note both versions on the scorecard and ask the candidate to reconcile them. Do not assume which version is right.

## Disqualifiers

Any one of these ends the process regardless of score. Record it and tell the hiring manager the same day.

- Fewer than 2 years of direct people management. Mentoring and tech-lead work without line responsibility do not count.
- Cannot describe their own individual contribution to a project they claim, after two follow-up prompts.
- Unwilling to take part in the on-call rotation.

## Red flags

These do not end the process on their own. Record them and raise them in the debrief.

- Speaks dismissively about former colleagues or managers.
- Shares a former employer's confidential customer data.
- Takes sole credit for a team outcome and does not correct it when asked who else was involved.

## Panel recommendation

After the panel loop, the recommendation is "hire" when at least 3 of the 4 panel interviewers score the candidate 3.0 or above.

## Notes

Free text. Quote the candidate where you can. Keep opinions about personality, accent, age, family plans or anything unrelated to the competencies above out of the record.
