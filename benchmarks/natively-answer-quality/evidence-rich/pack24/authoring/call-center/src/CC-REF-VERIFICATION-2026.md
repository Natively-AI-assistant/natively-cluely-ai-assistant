# Brindlewatch Account Verification Standard

Version 4.2 | Effective 15 June 2026
Owner: Odalys Ferreira-Novak, Director of Customer Care | Approved by: Trust & Security Desk
Applies to: every Customer Care agent on phone, chat and e-mail, at every tier

This Standard replaces all earlier caller-verification guidance, including section 2 of the Support Policy Handbook.

## 1. Why we verify

A Brindlewatch account controls cameras in and around someone's home. Letting the wrong person into it is the most serious mistake a Customer Care agent can make, and a sympathetic story is the usual way it happens. Verify first, help second, and treat the rules below as fixed however reasonable the caller sounds.

## 2. Standard verification

Before discussing anything specific to an account, the caller must correctly give 3 of the 5 identifiers below.

| Identifier | Notes |
|---|---|
| Full name on the account | As it is spelled on the account |
| Login e-mail address | The caller says it; the agent never reads it out or completes it |
| Billing postal code | |
| Last four digits of the payment card on file | Never ask for the full number, the expiry date or the security code |
| Serial number of a registered camera | On the label under the stand; begins with BW |

Never ask for a password.

A caller who gets an identifier wrong may try once more. After 2 failed attempts in the same contact, stop: the account is locked for phone and chat verification for 24 hours and the caller is pointed to Identity Review (section 5). Do not say which answer was wrong.

## 3. Step-up verification

Some actions need more than section 2. For these the agent sends a 6-digit one-time code to the phone number or e-mail address on file. The customer reads it back, and it expires after 10 minutes.

Step-up is required before:

- changing the login e-mail address;
- adding or removing an Authorised Contact;
- filing a recordings Deletion Request;
- changing the delivery address on an open order;
- closing the account.

## 4. Third-party callers

Only two kinds of people may be helped with an account: the account holder, and an Authorised Contact the account holder has registered. Everyone else is a third party - spouses, partners, children, parents, housemates, landlords and installers included - however much they know about the account and whether or not they live at the billing address.

With a third party, give general product and policy information only. Do not confirm whether an account exists, and do not act on one. Invite them to have the account holder call, or to be registered as an Authorised Contact.

Authorised Contacts. An account can have up to 2 Authorised Contacts. The account holder adds one in the app (Settings > Account > Authorised Contacts) or on a verified call after a step-up code. Each Authorised Contact chooses a 4-digit contact PIN and is verified by their own name plus that PIN.

| An Authorised Contact may | An Authorised Contact may not |
|---|---|
| Get troubleshooting help for the cameras | Change the login e-mail address or password, or receive a reset link |
| Check order status and billing status | Change the payment method |
| Ask for a carrier trace on an order | Delete recordings, request a refund or close the account |

Power of attorney, guardianship and the accounts of customers who have died are handled by Tier 2 Account Integrity, on documents. Tier 1 takes the caller's details and makes no change.

## 5. Customer cannot reach the e-mail on file

Verify with 3 of the 5 identifiers as usual, then send the step-up code by text message to the phone number on file. Once the code is confirmed, the customer can change the login e-mail in the app, or the agent can change it for them.

If there is no phone number on file either, start Identity Review (form IR-7). The customer uploads government photo ID and either proof of purchase or a photo of a camera's serial label. Tier 2 Account Integrity decides; tell the customer to allow up to 5 business days. A Tier 1 agent never changes a login e-mail without a confirmed step-up code or an approved IR-7.

## 6. Accessibility accommodations

Customers who are deaf or hard of hearing, or who have a speech, cognitive or motor disability, are verified to the same standard by a route that works for them.

Relay calls. A call placed through a telephone relay service or a video relay interpreter is the customer speaking. Do not ask the relay operator to verify themselves, allow extra time, and do not end the call because of silence.

Assisted calls. The account holder may have a helper - a relative, friend or carer - speak for them, provided the account holder is present and consents. Consent can be given by voice, or by replying YES to the consent message we send to the phone or e-mail on file. The helper may relay the account holder's identifiers. Consent covers that call only.

Changing channel. Any customer may ask to continue by live chat, or by e-mail to the address on file; offer this without being asked when hearing is the difficulty. One-time codes can be sent by text message instead of a voice call.

Where things are sent. An accommodation changes how we communicate, never where credentials go. Reset links, one-time codes and security notices are sent only to the contact details on file, including during an assisted call, and never to a helper's e-mail address or phone.

A helper who needs lasting access should be set up properly: as an Authorised Contact (section 4), or through a Shared Access invitation sent from the account holder's app, which gives view-only access to chosen cameras.

Longer appointments. Customers who need more time can book a 45-minute accessibility appointment, offered on Tuesday and Thursday afternoons.

## 7. Business accounts

A Brindlewatch for Business account is verified through the administrator named on it, using the same identifiers. Employees of the business who are not the administrator are third parties.

## 8. Ticket notes

Record which identifiers were used ("name, postal code, card"), never their values. Record whether a step-up code was confirmed and, on an assisted call, who the helper was and how consent was given.
