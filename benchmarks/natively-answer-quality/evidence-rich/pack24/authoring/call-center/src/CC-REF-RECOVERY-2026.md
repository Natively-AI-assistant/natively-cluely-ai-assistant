# Password and Account Recovery Procedure

Version 2.3 | Effective 20 April 2026
Owner: Bartholomew Nkemelu-Strand, Trust & Security Desk

## 1. Password resets

Agents cannot see, read out or set a customer's password. What an agent can do is trigger a reset e-mail from the support console; the customer can do the same with Forgot password on the sign-in screen.

- The link goes to the login e-mail address on file. Any exception to that is set by the Account Verification Standard, not by this procedure.
- The link expires 30 minutes after it is sent.
- The e-mail can take up to 5 minutes to arrive. Ask the customer to look in junk and promotions folders before sending another.
- No more than 5 reset e-mails can be sent to one account in 24 hours.

## 2. Customer cannot get into the e-mail on file

After standard verification, send a one-time code by text message to the phone number on file. With the code confirmed, the login e-mail can be changed to an address the customer uses now, and the reset is sent there.

With no phone number on file, the route is Identity Review (form IR-7), handled by Tier 2 Account Integrity. Tell the customer to allow up to 3 business days.

## 3. Two-step sign-in

Customers who turned on two-step sign-in were given 10 backup codes, each usable once. A customer who has lost the authenticator device and has no backup codes left goes through Identity Review. Agents cannot switch two-step sign-in off.

## 4. Lockouts

After 6 failed sign-in attempts the account locks for 15 minutes. Agents cannot lift the lock. Ask the customer to wait it out and then use a reset rather than guessing again.

## 5. Login e-mail changed without the customer's knowledge

Treat this as a possible account takeover.

What Tier 1 does: do not change the e-mail back, do not send a reset to either address, and do not remove devices. Escalate at once to the Trust & Security desk as Priority 1.

What Security does: freezes the account - signs out every session and pauses Shared Access - and then restores it to its rightful owner.

Two protections are already in place, and it helps to tell the customer about them:

- When a login e-mail is changed, the previous address receives a notice containing a "This wasn't me" link. The link works for 7 days and reverses the change immediately.
- For 72 hours after any change of login e-mail, sensitive actions are blocked: deleting recordings, removing cameras and changing the payment method.

## 6. New-device alerts

A sign-in from a phone, tablet or browser we have not seen before sends an alert e-mail to the account holder. The customer can review everything signed in under Settings > Account > Devices and use Sign out everywhere. An alert on its own is not an incident; an alert the customer cannot explain is, and goes to the Trust & Security desk.

## 7. Shared Access

The account holder can invite up to 4 guests to view chosen cameras. A guest can watch live view and clips but cannot change settings, delete anything or see billing. Each guest signs in with their own e-mail and password, and the account holder can withdraw an invitation at any time.

## 8. What to note on the ticket

Which route was used (reset e-mail, text code, Identity Review), the time the reset was triggered, and whether the customer confirmed they were signed in before the call ended.
