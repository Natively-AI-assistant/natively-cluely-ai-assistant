I don't have specific past experience loaded right now. I can frame this honestly as a small, relevant example if that matches my background:

Honestly, the sharpest signal was when I owned a payment reconciliation service that was hitting p99 latency in the tens of minutes during a quarterly close. I went and pulled traces for the slowest one percent of requests, and almost all of them were stuck waiting on a single batched write to a downstream ledger table that was contending for locks with the read path.

So the move I made was to split that one write into two paths. A fast path for the hot, recent entries using an append-only stream, and a slow path for backfill and reconciliation that didn't share locks with live traffic. I also added a circuit breaker on the downstream so a degradation couldn't cascade into the request path.

The result was a big drop in tail latency during the close window, and frankly more importantly, the on-call rotation stopped getting paged at 2am. The thing I'd point to as the actual learning is that latency cliffs at the tail are almost always contention or shared resources, not raw throughput, and the fix is usually partitioning, not more hardware.
