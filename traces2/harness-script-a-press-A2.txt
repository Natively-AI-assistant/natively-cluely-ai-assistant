I&apos;m a staff engineer on the payments orchestration platform, where I lead the merchant settlement and reconciliation pipeline. My team is five engineers plus two data folks, and we own the systems that take transactions from authorization all the way through to the ledger entries that show up in merchant dashboards. So that&apos;s ingestion, normalization, FX handling, dispute netting, and the daily reconciliation reports merchants actually look at. On the team setup side, I&apos;ve got two engineers focused on the streaming layer in Kafka, one on the batch reconciliation jobs, one more on the merchant-facing APIs, and the data folks handle the analytics warehouse and reporting. I&apos;ve been tech lead for about two years, so I split time between hands-on coding on the hot paths, design reviews for the rest of the team, and working with finance ops on the requirements side. Most of the impact I&apos;ve had there is around reliability. When I joined, our reconciliation jobs were running into timeout issues at the end of each month when transaction volume spiked, and finance ops was regularly flagging discrepancies that took days to track down. I rewrote the reconciliation engine to be idempotent and stream-based rather than batch, which cut our p95 settlement latency significantly and eliminated the month-end backlog. We also built an automated discrepancy detection layer that catches breaks at ingestion rather than downstream, so finance ops gets clean reports and only has to investigate real exceptions. That piece has saved them a lot of manual work and is the kind of cross-functional impact I enjoy most about the role.
