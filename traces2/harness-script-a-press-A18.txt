"Honestly, the system I'm proudest of is the reconciliation rewrite I led at Stripe. We were processing around 4.2 billion ledger entries a day, and the legacy pipeline was a Hadoop batch job with a 14-hour end-to-end lag. For a finance-adjacent surface, that kind of latency makes fraud, dispute, and settlement work painfully reactive.

I designed and shipped a streaming replacement on Kafka and Flink. The interesting parts weren't really the framework choice. They were the idempotency story, exactly-once semantics across the sink, and how we handled late-arriving events and backfills without double-counting. We also added a deterministic replay layer so finance and engineering could both reproduce any day's totals bit-for-bit from the raw stream.

Result was a p99 reconciliation latency drop from 14 hours to 38 minutes, and compute cost down about 61 percent, roughly 2.3 million a year. But the thing I actually value from that project is that it made reconciliation boring, which is exactly what you want for money-adjacent infrastructure."
