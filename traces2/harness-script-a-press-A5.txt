--- Raw model answer ---
The sharded RocksDB re-architecture took sustained write throughput from **1.1M points/sec to 8.4M points/sec** on the same hardware footprint, roughly a 7.6x improvement. The win came from three changes: sharding the keyspace by metric ID so writes hit independent RocksDB instances in parallel, switching to a group-commit log structure to amortize fsync overhead, and tuning compaction to stop the write-stall spikes that were capping ingest. The result was tail aggregation stopped being the bottleneck, and we could absorb the high-cardinality growth from the largest customers without adding nodes.
