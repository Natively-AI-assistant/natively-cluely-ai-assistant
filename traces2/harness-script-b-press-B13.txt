--- Raw model answer ---
So the decoder actually has two flavors of attention happening, not just self-attention. There's the self-attention layer, which is similar to the encoder's in that queries, keys, and values all come from the same previous decoder layer. But on top of that, there's an extra attention sub-layer the encoder doesn't have, called encoder-decoder attention. In that one, the queries come from the decoder, but the keys and values come from the encoder's output. That's what lets each decoder position look back at the entire input sequence.

The other big difference is masking. The decoder's self-attention is modified so that positions can't attend to future positions. Combined with shifting the output embeddings by one, this keeps the prediction autoregressive, meaning position $i$ can only depend on known outputs at positions before $i$.
