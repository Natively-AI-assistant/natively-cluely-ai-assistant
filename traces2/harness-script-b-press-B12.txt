--- Raw model answer ---
**Multi-head attention** keeps the total cost roughly the same as single-head attention because each head works on a smaller slice of the embedding rather than the full thing. So instead of one attention layer doing a $d_{model}$-dimensional projection, you do $h$ heads in parallel, each projecting into $d_{model}/h$ dimensions.

Concretely, a single dot-product attention takes $Q K^\top$ where the per-token vectors are $d_k$ wide, so the work scales like $d_k^2$ per head. With 8 heads at $d_k = 64$, each head does $64^2 = 4096$ units of work, and $8 \times 4096 = 32768$. Compare that to one head at full $d_{model} = 512$, which is $512^2 = 262144$. So multi-head is actually way cheaper per head, and the 8 heads together still come out cheaper than one full-width head.

The trick is the parameter matrices $W_i^Q, W_i^K, W_i^V$ still map from the full $d_{model}$, so each head sees the whole input, just in a lower-dimensional projection space. Then you concatenate the $h$ outputs and let $W^O$ mix them back into $d_{model}$. Net effect: similar parameter count, similar FLOPs, but the model gets to attend to information from $h$ different representation subspaces at once, which is what makes it more expressive than a single head of the same total width.
