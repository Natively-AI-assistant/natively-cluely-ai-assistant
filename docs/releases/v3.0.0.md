## Summary

Four weeks of work since v2.9.2: a notepad for every meeting, overlay buttons you write yourself, Connectors, notes you can share as a link, and follow-up emails in your own style.

## What's New

- Notepad: Type your own notes while Natively listens. Recipes such as What did I miss, Write tldr, List my todos and Write follow up email run from chips or from "/" in Ask, a live transcript sits beside your notes, and Ask reads the whole meeting once it ends.
- Your own overlay buttons: Describe a button in plain sentences (what it does, what to read, how the answer looks) and Natively turns it into an overlay action, next to the built-in ones. Pick the five buttons the overlay shows. In Settings → Overlay.
- Connectors: Send meeting notes to Gmail, Slack, Notion, Zapier, webhooks and the CRMs (HubSpot, Salesforce, Attio, Affinity, Pipedrive), with browser sign-in instead of tokens and auto-send rules. A local MCP server and API let your own tools read your meetings. Your notes go straight from your computer to the service, never through our servers. In Settings → Connectors.
- Share notes as a link: Send anyone a link to a meeting's notes and transcript, from the meeting's Share menu next to Copy and File.
- Follow-ups in your style: A button in the meeting header opens a compose sheet, and the follow-up email is written in your tone when you click, for older meetings too. Set the style per mode in Modes Manager.

## Improvements

- Natively knows your meeting app. It reads Zoom and Teams for the meeting ID, who is on the call and whether you are muted, follows the microphone the meeting app uses, and detects more meeting apps.
- Room speakers: people sharing one microphone are told apart in the transcript and notes, on Natively, Deepgram and Soniox speech.
- New models appear by themselves. Each provider's model list is fetched live, so a model released today can be picked today.
- Screenshots and page captures are remembered across hotkeys, quick actions and typed chat, and you can paste or drop images into the overlay.
- Long meetings keep their whole transcript, from the first minute to the last.

## Fixes

- Windows system audio: Capture that never started, went silent mid-meeting or played on another device now works, and connections through antivirus HTTPS scanning (Kaspersky and similar) no longer fail.
- Transcription: Meetings with no words transcribed on macOS and Windows, and local models on a fresh Mac or PC, are fixed. Soniox no longer goes deaf after a silent call start.
- Groq speech: Natively sends Groq longer pieces, so its per-request minimum no longer inflates your bill.
- Overlay: The first quick-action answer no longer leaves the window too short, and copy buttons work on Windows.
- Codex: A Codex answer the backend holds back is no longer cut off, and the default model is one a ChatGPT account can use.

## Technical

- The app is called Natively again on both platforms. 2.9.2's system process names (corespeechd on macOS, audiodg on Windows) are gone, and the data folder moves back by itself on first launch. Process Disguise in Settings still presents the app as Terminal, System Settings or Activity Monitor.
- Source maps are no longer shipped inside the app, so the download is smaller.

## macOS Installation (Signed Build)

Download the correct architecture `.dmg` or `.zip` file for your device (Apple Silicon or Intel).

- **For Apple Silicon (M1/M2/M3/M4):** Download the `arm64` build.
- **For Intel Macs:** Download the `x64` build.

Open the downloaded file, drag **Natively** to your **Applications** folder, and launch it.

## ⚠️ Windows Installation (Unsigned Build)

When running the installer on Windows, you might see a "Windows protected your PC" warning from Microsoft Defender SmartScreen saying it prevented an unrecognized app from starting.

Since this is an unsigned build, this is expected. You can safely ignore it by clicking **More info** and then **Run anyway**.
